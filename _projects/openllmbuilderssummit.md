The Open LLM Builders Summit is a recurring, invite-only gathering of the teams building open large language models worldwide, hosted by the [Swiss AI Initiative](https://www.swiss-ai.org/) through the [ETH AI Center](https://ai.ethz.ch) and [EPFL AI Center](https://ai.epfl.ch). I co-organize the series, which convenes roughly twice a year. Each edition brings together open-source and open-weight model initiatives, compute and infrastructure providers, researchers, and policymakers to move global collaboration on open models from aspiration to implementation.

## 3rd edition — Zurich, 30 September 2026

Hosted at the ETH AI Center as an official event of the [Zurich AI Festival 2026](https://luma.com/yz3g1757). The program features progress updates from major open-source and open-weight initiatives — including Nemotron (NVIDIA) and K2 (MBZUAI) — a poster session, breakout discussions, and a closing panel on global collaboration, with a focus on sustainable and competitive funding models for open AI. Speakers include Imanol Schlag, Martin Jaggi, Antoine Bosselut, Jeff Hammerbacher, Ahmad Chamma, and Matthias Bethge, among others.

## 2nd edition — Lausanne, 9 February 2026

Hosted at EPFL, with presentations from open-model builders including GLM (Z.ai) and Qwen (Alibaba). The central question: how can open LLMs scale globally without losing the agility, diversity, and innovation that define them? Breakout sessions and a closing plenary led by the Scientific Committee — Antoine Bosselut (EPFL), Joost VandeVondele (CSCS), Imanol Schlag (ETH Zurich), and Valentina Pyatkin (Ai2) — surfaced concrete themes: sharing the full training pipeline rather than just weights (data mixtures, filtering, evaluation, negative results), moving from open models to open *systems* with serving infrastructure and feedback loops, and navigating regulatory and geopolitical fragmentation.

## 1st edition — Geneva, July 2025

The inaugural International Open-Source LLM Builders Summit gathered around 50 leading global initiatives and organizations dedicated to open-source LLMs and trustworthy AI. Participants got a first preview of [Apertus](https://www.apertus-ai.org/), Switzerland's fully open, multilingual language model developed by EPFL, ETH Zurich, and CSCS, which was released under Apache 2.0 later that summer.
